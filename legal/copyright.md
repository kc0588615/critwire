## Sending a notice

If you believe something on critwire.com infringes a copyright you own or are authorized to act for, send a written notice that includes all of the following:

1. Your physical or electronic signature.
2. The copyrighted work you say is infringed. If there are several, a representative list is enough.
3. The material you say infringes it, and where it is: the page address (URL) of each item, so we can find it.
4. Your name, postal address, phone number and email address.
5. A statement that you believe in good faith that the use isn't authorized by the copyright owner, its agent or the law.
6. A statement that the information in your notice is accurate and, under penalty of perjury, that you're the copyright owner or authorized to act for the owner.

A notice that leaves something out may not be valid, and we may ask you to complete it.

## Where to send it

Our designated agent is [DMCA agent: pending registration]. Until the agent is registered, send notices to admin@critwire.com.

## What we do with a notice

When we receive a valid notice, we remove the material or disable access to it promptly. We tell the studio whose portal it was on, and send them a copy of the notice. Players send feedback without an account, so we can't usually reach the person who sent a piece of feedback.

## Counter-notices

If your material was removed and you believe that was a mistake, or that the material was misidentified, you can send a counter-notice to the same address. It must include:

1. Your physical or electronic signature.
2. The material that was removed, and where it appeared before it was removed.
3. A statement, under penalty of perjury, that you believe in good faith the material was removed or disabled as a result of a mistake or misidentification.
4. Your name, postal address and phone number.
5. A statement that you consent to the jurisdiction of the U.S. federal district court for the judicial district where your address is, or, if your address is outside the United States, any judicial district in which we can be found, and that you'll accept service of process from the person who sent the original notice, or their agent.

## Restoring material

When we receive a valid counter-notice, we send a copy to the person who sent the original notice. We restore the material 10 to 14 business days after we receive the counter-notice, unless they tell us first that they've filed a court action to stop the infringement.

## Don't make false claims

Only send a notice or counter-notice if you're sure it's accurate. Under U.S. law, someone who knowingly misrepresents that material infringes, or that it was removed by mistake, can be liable for the damages that causes, including costs and lawyers' fees.
