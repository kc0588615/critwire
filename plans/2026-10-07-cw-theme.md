# Mission: critwire's new look, the cw theme

This file is the mission's state. Agents: follow "In a mission session" in `~/.claude/CLAUDE.md`, and keep this file current.

## Brief


### Goal

Critwire's whole UI and brand move to the **cw** design language, which Danby made in Graphical (graphicalui.com, theme revision `bittw6orbj7l`), in light and dark.

1. **The guidance is installed in the repo.**
   - `GUI.md` and `gui/` are at the repo root, and the skills `graphical-ui`, `graphical-convert` and `graphical-audit` are under `.agents/skills/`.
   - `AGENTS.md` gains a short pointer, with nothing else in it replaced: "For UI work, follow GUI.md and use graphical-ui, graphical-convert, or graphical-audit as appropriate." The pointer also gives the skills' paths.
2. **cw is defined once, as named tokens, in critwire's existing theme system.**
   - The tokens cover cw's colours, neutrals, text steps, spacing, radii, borders, shadows, motion, focus and font roles, for both modes.
   - The theme system means the Tailwind v4 `@theme` in `globals.css`, the CSS variables of each surface, and Payload's admin variables.
   - Components use the named tokens. Literal values appear only in the token definitions.
3. **Critwire's own surfaces are fully converted, in light and dark:**
   - The home page `/`.
   - `signup`, `onboarding`, `verify`, `forgot-password`, `legal/*` (including `legal/accept`), `report-abuse` and the 404.
   - The share kit, and the shadcn `Button`.
4. **Critwire's brand is cw.**
   - The wordmark, the favicon (`.svg` and `.ico`), and the admin `Logo` and `Icon`.
   - A cw OG image, replacing the template's `public/website-template-OG.webp`. It's the default for any page without its own image (`src/utilities/generateMeta.ts`, `mergeOpenGraph.ts`).
   - The share kit's button images and live badge.
   - The auth and contact-form emails.
   - The old brand (`brand.css`: yellow `#f6d33c`, ink `#1d1f55`, the Anybody wordmark) is gone.
5. **The Payload admin is cw.**
   - Payload's own UI is themed through its theme variables (colours, font, radius) in both of its modes.
   - Critwire's custom admin views are fully converted: the sign-in, forgot and reset views, the dashboard (`BeforeDashboard`), the Issues kanban, the Share tab (with its Discord tab), and the legal gate.
6. **Studio portals and embeds sit on a cw base, in the studio's colours.**
   - cw's structure becomes the portal's: type scale, spacing, radii, flat surfaces, and the styling of every component, state and portal.
   - **cw dark becomes the default portal theme.**
   - Each studio's saved theme (colours, `typography`, `shape`, `density`, `motion`) still applies on top.
   - This covers `/g/<slug>` and all its pages, and the board, updates and floating-button embeds.
7. **Every component in scope has a status:** converted, already matches, intentionally preserved, or blocked, each with evidence or a reason. A `graphical-audit` pass has found and fixed the remaining gaps.

### Why

Danby is rebranding critwire around cw, so that the product, its admin and the base of every studio's portal look like one deliberately designed thing. Studios still own their colours. That's the white-label promise ("a hub in the studio's colours"), and it doesn't change.

Popups, fields and cards still need visible edges in dark mode. Use a cw neutral step that keeps them visible, and record it.
  - cw is flat by design. The 0-opacity shadows are intended; don't "fix" them.
- **Portals: cw is the base, and the studio's theme wins.**
  - `siteThemeSchema`'s contrast rules don't change.
  - The new `DEFAULT_THEME_COLORS` are cw dark: background `#000000`, foreground `#ffffff`, mutedForeground `#a1a3a6`, surface `#1f2022`, accent `#31c3e8`, accentForeground `#000000`, success `#76ef6b`, warning `#ffa344`, error `#ff5263`, and a border from the dark neutrals that stays visible. Check that they pass the schema.
  - cw light can't be a portal default: its accent on white is 2.1:1, under the schema's 3:1.
  - The default `typography` uses cw's font. The other choices keep their display voices (Archivo, Young Serif, Science Gothic, in `src/components/game/theme/fonts.ts`).

- **Fonts.** cw names "GT Standard M", a paid font. Danby chose a free look-alike instead, which is a deliberate substitution, not a silent one.
  - Pick the closest face that has a licence for commercial use (OFL or similar), weights 400, 500 and 700, and latin and latin-ext. Self-host it with `next/font`, with no runtime third-party requests.
  - Keep the family behind the cw font-role tokens (`--font-ui`, `--font-brand`, `--font-editorial`), so a licensed GT Standard can replace it in one place later.
  - The data role, "Sidebar Geist Mono", is Geist Mono (OFL), self-hosted the same way.
  - Record both choices in GUI.md's "Local decisions" and in the Summary.
  - Remove fonts that are no longer used (`src/fonts.ts`) only after checking that nothing uses them.
- **Icons.** cw names the "Central" icon set, which is paid. Keep `lucide-react` (already installed, and outlined), at cw's `--icon-stroke-width` of 2. Don't add an icon package.

**Problems, ranked.**

1. **High. No single definition of the design language.** Every stylesheet above carries its own literals: five palettes and four type scales, including Tailwind's defaults. This breaks DRY and the brief's "literal values only in token definitions", and it is why the light/dark work can't be done once. *Fix:* one token source (§2).
2. **High. Critwire's own pages have no dark mode.** `.cw-root` is light only, and the admin's sign-in is restyled only for light. The brief requires `prefers-color-scheme` on critwire's pages and both Payload modes. *Fix:* `light-dark()` tokens plus a `color-scheme` per surface (§2).
3. **High. Three buttons for one role.** `.cw-btn` (`marketing.css:61`), shadcn `Button` (`button.tsx:9`, utilities) and `.fs-btn-primary` (`portal.css:181`). The links are duplicated the same way: `.cw-link`/`.cw-tap` against `.fs-link`/`.fs-tap`. This is DRY. `.cw-account` already proves that the `.fs-*` control layer works on critwire's pages. *Fix:* one control layer, `.fs-*` on `--fs-*` roles, which `.cw-root` defines. `Button`'s variants delegate to it.
4. **Medium. The admin compiles a second Tailwind** (`issues/kanban.css`) only to use default-palette utilities. E2E selectors depend on those utilities: `admin-triage.spec.ts:26,29,37,40,55` use `div.w-72`, `div.min-h-20` and `p.font-semibold`. *Fix:* plain BEM CSS like the other admin views, and move the selectors to the BEM hooks (markup changed, the meaning stays).
5. **Medium. Admin fonts.** The next/font variable `--font-body` has the same name as Payload's own `--font-body`, and the admin can't put a class on `<html>` without editing the generated layout. *Fix:* use distinct face variables (`--font-cw-sans`, `--font-cw-mono`) and Payload's documented `RootLayout` `htmlProps` prop (present in 3.85.2, `layouts/Root/index.d.ts`).
6. **Medium. The portal default lives in the database, and partial saved themes depend on it.** The theme fields' `defaultValue` (`GameProjects/theme.ts:40-48`) becomes the Postgres column default (migration `20260930_072559_portal_theme`). Rows created so far store the old defaults literally (`#1f2030` …, `modern`), so a new `DEFAULT_THEME_COLORS` restyles only new games, the `DEFAULT_THEME` pages (portal 404, `/unavailable`) and the embed's dark neutral palette. But a row can also hold an unset slot (`null` or `''`, from a cleared field), and `mergeTheme` (`theme.ts`) fills it from whatever `DEFAULT_THEME` is at render time. Changing the defaults can therefore push a saved custom palette under the schema's floor, and `resolveProjectTheme` (`projectTheme.ts:15-23`) then drops the studio's whole theme for the default. Example (Astra): surface `#404040` with `mutedForeground` unset is 4.62:1 against the old fallback `#a9acc2` and 4.10:1 against cw's `#a1a3a6`. Changing the defaults also needs a schema change (column defaults and a new enum value), which can't be one migration: Payload runs each migration file in its own transaction (`migrate.js:69-87`), and Postgres refuses a new enum value in the transaction that added it. *Fix:* §7 (two migrations; the second pins every unset slot to the old default it renders with today).
7. **Medium. Duplicated brand and email literals.** OG URL ×2; email `main`/`container` objects ×2 (`AuthLinkEmail.tsx:11-26`, `ContactFormEmail.tsx:23-38`). *Fix:* one `DEFAULT_OG_IMAGE`, one `email/templates/styles.ts`, both built from the token source.
8. **Low. The portal's type is a parallel scale.** Fluid clamps multiplied by each voice's `scale` and `leading`. *Fix:* re-express on cw's text steps (§7).
9. **Low. The portal root sets no `color-scheme`**, so native `<select>` popups, scrollbars and Turnstile `auto` render light on dark palettes. *Fix:* `themeStyle` sets it from the palette's luminance, with the threshold shared with `embedPalettes` (DRY).
10. **Low. The screenshot harness has no contrast probe.** The brief's quality floor lists one. The marketing focus probe hard-codes `var(--cw-ink)` (`probes.ts:177`).
11. **Low, pre-existing, out of scope. The save check and the stored theme can disagree.** `validateProjectTheme` (`GameProjects/theme.ts:63-78`) validates `mergeTheme(originalDoc.theme, data.theme)`, where an explicit `null` or `''` in the submission keeps the original value, but Payload stores the clear. A studio can therefore save a theme that renders differently from the one validated, on any default palette. Fixing it changes what a save accepts, which the brief rules out (behaviour). Recorded as G8 with a suggested handoff item.

**Fine as they are, and kept:** `siteThemeSchema` and its WCAG pairs; the `--kit-*` adapter; `paletteVars`/`radiusVars`, shared by the portal and the embed; the embed's boot script and mode handling; the share images' text-as-paths approach; the `FormField`/`FormNotice` semantics (the notice text sits beside a status shape); the 44 px tap-target floor.

### 2. Token architecture

**The single source.** `src/lib/theme/cw.ts` holds cw as typed data, transcribed from `gui/themes/cw.md`: text steps, spacing, radii, border widths, weights, font fallbacks, motion, `color-1…4`, status colours, and `neutral-1…10` for light and dark. It is the only file with cw literals. A pure `cwTokensCSS()` (`src/lib/theme/tokensCss.ts`) renders it. Two generators, both run by hand, with their outputs committed; neither runs in `prebuild` or `build`, so the build stays fast and deterministic:
- `pnpm generate:theme` (tsx, `src/lib/theme/generate.ts`) writes only `src/styles/cw-tokens.css`, with a "generated, edit `cw.ts`" header: every primitive as a CSS variable. Mode-dependent values are written once as `light-dark(<light>, <dark>)`, for example `--neutral-3: light-dark(#eef0f3, #2a2b2d)`, so both modes stay together, as `theme-contract.md` asks.
- `pnpm generate:brand` (tsx, `src/lib/theme/brand.ts`) writes the brand files of §8 (`favicon.svg`, `favicon.ico`, `og.png`) through sharp and the generator-only font. It's rerun only when `cw.ts`'s brand colours or the wordmark change.

A new int test, `tests/int/cw-tokens.int.spec.ts`, fails when the committed CSS differs from `cwTokensCSS()`, or when `v1.js`'s colour pair differs from `cw.ts`. E2E can't see source drift. `prebuild` runs it beside `embed-loader` (one `vitest run` with both files); it reads files and renders strings, with no sharp and no fonts.

**Roles, hand-written, no literals.** `src/styles/cw.css` has no `@import` (Dart Sass passes a plain-CSS `@import` through and hoists it, and css-loader would then resolve it from `src/app/(payload)/`, not `src/styles/`). It assumes the tokens are already loaded, and defines:
- the `-transparent` variants as `color-mix(in srgb, var(--x) 20%, transparent)` (cw's own rule);
- the `--cte-*` roles;
- `--border-default-color: light-dark(rgb(0 0 0 / 0.1), var(--neutral-4))` (departure D4);
- `--border-shadow-{none,s,m,l}: 0 0 0 var(--border-*) var(--border-default-color)`;
- `--shadow-{s,m,l}` (0 opacity, flat as designed);
- `--focus-ring-color: color-mix(in srgb, var(--neutral-10) 50%, transparent)` and `--focus-ring-outline: var(--border-l) solid var(--focus-ring-color)`, which equal the snapshot's black and white rings (the colour is its own role so the focus probe can resolve the same expression, §9);
- the font roles: `--font-ui|brand|editorial: var(--font-cw-sans), -apple-system, BlinkMacSystemFont, sans-serif` and `--font-data: var(--font-cw-mono), monospace`.

**Mode wiring.** Each surface owns its `color-scheme`, and `light-dark()` follows it. No selector block is repeated per mode.

| Surface | How the mode is set |
|---|---|
| Critwire's pages (`(frontend)`) | `export const viewport = { colorScheme: 'light dark' }` in the layout, plus `.cw-root { color-scheme: light dark }`. Follows `prefers-color-scheme`, with no toggle. |
| Admin | Payload's own `html[data-theme='dark'] { color-scheme: dark }`. Light is `normal`, so `light-dark()` resolves light. `cw.css` itself never sets `color-scheme`, so Payload's light/dark/auto switch keeps working. |
| Portals | `themeStyle` adds `colorScheme: dark` or `light` from the palette's background luminance. The helper is extracted from `embedPalettes`' `DARK_LUMINANCE`. |
| Embeds | Unchanged: `.cw-embed` sets `color-scheme` per `data-mode`. |

Verified: Tailwind 4.3.2 runs Lightning CSS with `exclude: Features.LightDark` (`@tailwindcss/node/dist/index.js`), so `light-dark()` ships natively and isn't polyfilled into per-root variables.

**How each consumer reaches the tokens.**

| Consumer | How |
|---|---|
| Tailwind (`globals.css`) | Right after `@import 'tailwindcss'` and before `marketing.css`/`portal.css`: `@import '../../styles/cw-tokens.css';` then `@import '../../styles/cw.css';` (unlayered). `@theme` resets the default design namespaces (`--color-*`, `--spacing-*`, `--text-*`, `--font-*`, `--font-weight-*`, `--leading-*`, `--tracking-*`, `--radius-*`, `--shadow-*`, `--ease-*`, `--animate-*`: `initial`) and keeps the breakpoints. ### 6. Fonts

The call is `Inter({ axes: ['opsz'], display: 'swap', subsets: ['latin', 'latin-ext'], variable: '--font-cw-sans' })`, with no `weight` (variable). `next/font` ships only the `wght` axis unless `axes` asks for more, the same way `src/fonts.ts` asks Anybody for `wdth` today; without `opsz` the optical-size argument below does nothing.

Why Inter is the closest free face to GT Standard M:
- It is a neutral neo-grotesque in the same "Standard"/Akzidenz lineage, with horizontal terminals, a straight-legged R and a large x-height (0.546).
- It was built for UI text at 13–15 px and has tabular figures.
- Its optical-size axis mirrors GT Standard's size-specific cuts. Text steps get the text cut, and the 33/44 px headings get the tighter display cut automatically (`font-optical-sizing: auto`), so cw's 0 em tracking still looks right at xl and xxl.
- Rejected: Geist (more geometric), Instrument Sans and Schibsted Grotesk (more character than the neutral original), Public Sans (American Gothic).

**Geist Mono** (`next/font/google`, OFL, `latin` and `latin-ext`, `preload: false`) fills the data role ("Sidebar Geist Mono").

`src/fonts.ts` exports `cwSans` (`variable: '--font-cw-sans'`), `cwMono` (`'--font-cw-mono'`) and `cwFontVariables`. That class string goes on `<html>` in the `(frontend)` and `(public)` layouts and, in the admin, through `RootLayout htmlProps={{ className: cwFontVariables }}`. The faces sit only behind the role tokens, so a licensed GT Standard replaces them by changing `src/fonts.ts` to `next/font/local`. No local family names go in the stack (consistent rendering, no fingerprinting).

**Removed:** Atkinson Hyperlegible Next and Anybody. The only consumers today are `src/fonts.ts`, both layouts, `Logo.tsx`/`Icon.tsx`, `brand.css:18`, `marketing.css:35,42,406`, `portal.css:33` and `globals.css:86`. All of them are converted. Removal comes last, after `grep -rn "font-body\|font-critwire\|bodyFont\|critwireFont" src` finds nothing but Payload's own `--font-body` mapping.

**Kept:** Archivo, Young Serif and Science Gothic (`game/theme/fonts.ts`).

**Server-side images:** `src/lib/share/fonts/` adds the static `Inter-Medium.ttf` (runtime) and `InterDisplay-Bold.ttf` (`generate:brand` only), from the Inter 4.1 release, with its `OFL.txt`. `DejaVuSans.ttf` and its `LICENSE` stay as the per-character fallback:
- `glyphPath` sets each character in Inter Medium and falls back to DejaVu Sans for any character Inter has no glyph for (`charToGlyph(…).index === 0`), advancing x run by run. `centredBaseline` measures Inter's cap height.
- Why: a badge's version label is studio-written. Measured below U+3000, Inter 4.1 covers 2,071 code points and DejaVu 4,700; Hebrew, Arabic, Armenian and Georgian are DejaVu-only (neither has CJK). Without the fallback those labels would turn into boxes, a regression from today.
- `next.config.ts` traces both `Inter-Medium.ttf` and `DejaVuSans.ttf` on its three routes (`/buttons/*`, `/g/*/badge.svg`, `/g/*/badge.png`); `InterDisplay-Bold.ttf` is never traced.

The embeds keep the host's font: `.cw-embed` sets all four role tokens to `var(--cw-font, system-ui, sans-serif)` (D13).

- **Saved themes keep their look and stay valid:**
  - The enum gains a value and loses none; colours are validated as before; the schema's rules don't change.

  - A row with an unset slot (finding 6) is completed by migration B below with the value it renders with today, so it too resolves exactly as today.
- **Two ordered migrations** (D25), each generated with `pnpm payload migrate:create`, so each carries its own `.json` snapshot. Payload commits each migration file's transaction before running the next, on `pnpm payload migrate` and on boot through `prodMigrations`, so the enum value is committed before anything uses it.
  - **A, `cw_typography_standard`.** Generated after only adding `'standard'` to `siteTypographySchema` (first in the list, with the default still `'modern'`), so it holds just `ALTER TYPE "public"."enum_game_projects_theme_typography" ADD VALUE 'standard' BEFORE 'modern';` (if the generator writes a type rebuild instead, replace it with that one statement; the snapshot stays as generated). `down` is a no-op with a comment: Postgres can't drop an enum value without recreating the type, and B's `down` leaves no row using it.
  - **B, `cw_portal_defaults`.** Generated after switching `DEFAULT_THEME_COLORS` and the `typography` default. It holds the generated `ALTER TABLE "game_projects" ALTER COLUMN … SET DEFAULT` for the ten colour columns and `theme_typography` (`'standard'`), plus one hand-written data statement that completes saved themes:
    - `UPDATE "game_projects" SET "theme_colors_<key>" = COALESCE(NULLIF("theme_colors_<key>", ''), '<old default>')` for the ten colours, and `"theme_typography" = COALESCE("theme_typography", 'modern')`, `WHERE` any of those eleven is null or `''`, `RETURNING "id"`. The old values are literals, copied from `20260930_072559_portal_theme`'s column defaults (= the base's `DEFAULT_THEME`), because a migration is a frozen snapshot and must not import `theme.ts`.
    - `null` and `''` are exactly what `mergeTheme`'s `isSet` treats as unset, so each written value is the one the row renders with today. Rows with every slot set aren't touched; set values are never overwritten. Raw SQL leaves `updated_at` alone.
    - `shape`, `density` and `motion` aren't touched: their defaults don't change.
    - The completed ids are logged with `payload.logger.info`, as `backfill_media_folder_tenants` does.
    - `down` restores the old column defaults and sets `theme_typography = 'modern'` where it's `'standard'`, so the old code parses every row. Completed slots stay: they hold the values the old code fell back to anyway.
  - Then `pnpm generate:types`, and a third `migrate:create` must come out blank (no drift).
- **Why the backfill is correct.** Today a row renders `parse(merge(OLD, row))`, where `merge` fills each unset slot from `OLD`. B writes `OLD`'s value into exactly those slots, so afterwards `merge(NEW, row') = row' = merge(OLD, row)`, and the schema parses it the same way, because its only change is a wider enum. New games are complete from the start, because Payload's `defaultValue` (and the column default) store cw dark.
- **How it's proven (upgrade rehearsal).** E2E can't: `e2e:server` runs `payload migrate:fresh` on an empty database, so no row exists when B runs. The proof follows the repo's precedent for data migrations (`architecture-pass` Step 17c, a psql proof on the mission database), with its files under `/srv/critter-ai/agent-state/missions/cw-theme/proofs/`:
  1. Precondition: the mission database `critwire_m_cw_theme` is still at the base schema (23 migrations, no `dev` row), with A and B pending in `migrate:status`. Nothing in this mission dev-pushes to it; E2E and screenshots use `_e2e`.
  2. Insert fixtures by SQL (`proofs/theme-backfill.sql`): one tenant and five game projects. P1 is Astra's case: surface `#404040`, `mutedForeground` null, typography null, the rest the old defaults. P2 is P1 with `mutedForeground` `''`. P3 is a complete custom palette (Riso's values). P4 holds the old defaults (G5). P5 has `shape`, `density` and `motion` null.
  3. Negative control, before migrating: a tsx check under the new code (`siteThemeSchema.safeParse(mergeTheme(row))` on each row) fails P1 and P2 on `mutedForeground` (4.10:1). This shows the backfill is what fixes them. (Already checked for P1 against the real `siteThemeSchema`: it parses over the old defaults and fails over cw's with `mutedForeground on surface has contrast 4.10:1`.)
  4. `pnpm payload migrate`. Then: P1 and P2 hold `#a9acc2` and `modern`; P3, P4 and P5 are unchanged byte for byte; `updated_at` is unchanged everywhere; no row has a null or `''` in the eleven columns; the log names exactly P1 and P2; `migrate:status` lists A before B.
  5. The same tsx check passes on all five, and P1's resolved colours equal its pre-migration `COALESCE` with the old defaults.
  6. Delete the fixtures. Save the SQL, the before and after rows and the migrate output as `proofs/theme-backfill.log`.
- **Brief note.** The brief says saved themes "stay valid without a migration". That holds for complete rows. For partial rows it can't hold while the fallback palette changes, unless the code keeps the old palette as a second fallback forever (rejected below). B changes no studio's look: it writes down the values each partial row already renders with. Recorded as D23, for the owner's review at merge (handoff H20).

### 11. Risks, gaps and decisions

**Gaps (unresolved; for the Summary):**
- **G1.** Payload's built-in field boundaries use `--theme-elevation-150`. As n4 they're 1.35:1 (light) and 1.39–1.79:1 (dark), close to Payload's stock 1.37:1, so this isn't a regression. The variable mapping can't fix them: elevation-150 appears 90 times in Payload's compiled CSS, and only 22 of those rules are input edges. The rest fill pills, pill and subtle buttons, cells and the shimmer, which an n7 value would turn dark grey. The only real fix is overriding Payload's ~22 input selectors, which the brief rules out. The minimal-template views get the field edge, and no admin shot shows a built-in field outside them (§9). Handoff H21: the owner decides whether to allow one scoped override rule for Payload's input selectors. It blocks nothing.
- **G2.** Resolved by D26 (three rules; it was "Payload's light-mode error text at 3.16:1").
- **G3.** cw's `controlSize` has no resolved values in the snapshot, so the checkbox box size stays native (D9).
- **G4.** Native `<select>` popups and the Turnstile widget are external and drawn by the OS or Cloudflare.
- **G5.** Existing games that store the old defaults (`#1f2030` palette, `modern`) keep them, and migration B pins any unset slot to the old default too (D23). Handoff H20: the owner decides whether a later migration moves rows equal to the old default onto cw dark. It blocks nothing.
- **G6.** In light palettes, the status marks for success and warning are faint (1.47 and 1.99:1). The text label carries the meaning.
- **G7.** The Kanban/Table switch has no `aria-pressed`. That's a semantics change, out of scope.
- **G8.** Pre-existing (finding 11): `validateProjectTheme` validates the original value where a submission clears a colour, but the clear is what's stored. So a studio can save a theme that renders differently from the one checked, and if it fails, the portal falls back to the default theme (reported to Sentry). It doesn't affect existing rows (B completes them) and isn't caused by this mission. Fixing it changes what a save accepts, which is out of scope. Handoff H22: validate the stored result, so a clear falls back to the default and is checked. It blocks nothing.

## Failure modes

The lists below are specifications, written before the code they check.

**The token source: `tests/int/cw-tokens.int.spec.ts`.** It's written in S4 before `cw.ts`; T7 is added in S20 before `v1.js` changes. It fails when:
- **T1.** `src/styles/cw-tokens.css` differs from `cwTokensCSS()`: a hand edit, or a stale file after `cw.ts` changed.
- **T2.** A primitive in `cw.ts` differs from the snapshot in `gui/themes/cw.md`, through a transcription error. The test reads two parts of the snapshot:
  - the Foundations JSON: text steps (size, line height, letter spacing), spacing, radii, borders, weights per role, and motion (durations, easings, press distance, popup scale);
  - both modes' CSS-variable tables: `--neutral-1…10`, `--color-1…4`, `--success`, `--warning`, `--error` and `--cte-accent-text`.
  
  Departures live only in the roles in `cw.css`, never in `cw.ts`, so the comparison is exact.
- **T3.** A mode-dependent variable isn't written as `light-dark(<light>, <dark>)`, or its two modes are swapped.
- **T4.** The test passes vacuously because a parser read too little. It must find:
  - 7 text steps, 8 spacing steps, 7 radii and 4 borders;
  - 4 colours and 3 status colours;
  - 10 neutrals in each mode.
- **T5.** An entry point loads the roles before the tokens, or through a nested import (Fable MF2, Astra MF3). Required:
  - **`globals.css`** imports `../../styles/cw-tokens.css`, then `../../styles/cw.css`, straight after `tailwindcss` and before `./marketing.css`.
  - **`custom.scss`** `@use`s `../../styles/cw-tokens`, then `../../styles/cw`, before any other rule.
  - **`cw.css`** contains no `@import`.
- **T6.** `cw.css` sets `color-scheme`, which would override Payload's light/dark/auto switch.
- **T7.** `public/embed/v1.js`'s `COLOURS` aren't light `[CW.light n1, CW.light n10]` and dark `[CW.dark n1, CW.dark n10]` (S20).

The generator's determinism isn't in the test. S4 and the end-of-mission run check it by running `pnpm generate:theme` and diffing.

The check fails when:
