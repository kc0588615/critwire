Measure in metres and cut one layer by another: areas and lengths on the ellipsoid, ST_DWithin and buffers, ST_Intersection and ST_Difference, and how far two separately drawn maps are from fitting. Everything runs in your own PostgreSQL with PostGIS, on the lab tables (the setup page).

Concept

How to measure lengths, areas and distances in metres, and how to cut one layer by another:

  • Measure on geography (Coordinate reference systems, g1). Cast first, and ST_Length of a line, ST_Perimeter and ST_Area of a polygon, and ST_Distance between two shapes answer in metres and square metres, on the ellipsoid. On SRID 4326 geometry they answer in degrees.
  • "Within d?" is ST_DWithin(a, b, d); on geography, d is in metres. It gives the same answer as ST_Distance(a, b) <= d, but it starts with a bounding-box test that a spatial index (Indexes and query plans, d3) can answer, and measures only the shapes that pass. ST_Distance(a, b) < d measures every pair. The index must be on the expression the query compares: casting a geometry column to geography in the query leaves the column's geometry index unused (Spatial indexes, g5, shows the plans).
  • A buffer is the area within a distance of a shape, as a polygon: ST_Buffer(g, d), ArcGIS Pro's Buffer. On geography, PostGIS picks a flat system that suits the shape (a UTM zone, or a polar Lambert azimuthal one), buffers there in metres and turns the result back into longitude and latitude. Curves are drawn as straight segments, 8 to a quarter circle by default, so the polygon falls a little inside the true distance. To ask whether something lies within d, use ST_DWithin, which is exact and faster; draw a buffer when you need the area itself, to measure it, map it or cut another layer with it.
  • Overlay builds new shapes from two inputs' edges. ST_Intersection(a, b) keeps what both cover (ArcGIS Pro's Intersect, or Clip when b is one shape); ST_Difference(a, b) keeps the part of a outside b (Erase); ST_Union merges shapes into one, and as an aggregate it merges a column's rows (Dissolve). Overlays are much slower than predicates, so let a predicate the index answers pick the pairs (JOIN … ON ST_Intersects(…)) and overlay only those. An overlay can return lines and points where shapes only touch: ST_CollectionExtract(g, 2) keeps the lines, 3 the polygons. An invalid input can stop it with a TopologyException (Loading and validating shapes, g2).
  • Two maps never fit exactly. Layers drawn, or simplified, apart disagree along every line they share. The lab's ecoregions were simplified one at a time to 0.05°, about 5 km, and the country outlines on their own, so neighbouring ecoregions overlap in places and pull apart in others, and the ecoregions stop short of the outline's coast. Cut by an outline, the pieces add up to a little more or less than the whole: an overlay should report by how much, since that is the error any area it gives carries.
  • Valid isn't a coverage (g2). Each ecoregion here is a valid shape; ST_IsValid checks one row at a time and can't see that two rows overlap. Only a test across pairs finds it, the one g2 ran on the country outlines.

Worked example

How much of Costa Rica does each ecoregion cover? Cut each ecoregion that touches the outline by it, measure the piece on the ellipsoid, and divide by the outline's area:

Seven ecoregions reach into Costa Rica, led by the Isthmian-Atlantic moist forests (17,288 km², 33.7 %). Five are moist or dry broadleaf forest; the other two are mangroves along the coasts, Southern Mesoamerican Pacific (637 km²) and Mesoamerican Gulf-Caribbean (198 km²). ST_Intersects in the ON clause picks the ecoregions, with the index; ST_Intersection in the SELECT cuts only those seven. The shares add up to 97.3 %, not 100: the Lab asks why.

Lab

  1. Do the pieces add up to the country? The outline's area, the pieces' sum, and the part of the outline that no ecoregion covers (the outline less the union of the ecoregions):

    {{block:g4-gap}}

    The pieces add up to 49,908 km² of the outline's 51,303, and 2,070 km² lie in no ecoregion. Had the pieces been a partition, the two would add up to the outline exactly; they add up to 51,978, about 675 km² too many. So the pieces both leave gaps and overlap. About half of the gap lies within 5 km of the sea, where the ecoregions stop short of the outline's coast; the rest is slivers inland, where two neighbouring ecoregions both pulled back from their shared border.

  2. Where do the pieces overlap? Each pair of pieces whose interiors share an area (g2's '2********' test), with its DE-9IM matrix, and a total row first:

    {{block:g4-overlaps}}

    Ten pairs overlap, 678.2 km² in all: the 675 km² that step 1's pieces counted twice (a few square kilometres lie in three ecoregions at once, and come into three pairs). The largest are between neighbours along long borders: the Isthmian-Atlantic moist forests and the Talamancan montane forests (181.7 km²), the seasonal moist and the dry forests (143.8). Each is a thin strip along a border that each ecoregion, simplified on its own, drew in a slightly different place. Compare the matrices with g2's 2F2F11FF2, a territory wholly inside its country: here the third and seventh characters are 2 too, so each piece also reaches outside the other. These are partial overlaps between shapes that are each valid: the layer isn't a coverage, and nothing about one row says so. Pieces that overlap are counted twice by any sum over them, as step 1's was; a partition (each overlap given to one ecoregion, each gap to the nearest) would need rules of its own, and the honest report is the overlay's error: here about 1.3 % of the country in overlaps and 4.0 % in gaps.

  3. Costa Rica's land borders, their length, and the records within 10 km of each, by the code GBIF gave them:

    {{block:g4-border}}

    ST_Touches finds the outlines that share a border with Costa Rica's but no interior: Nicaragua and Panama. The border is where their two boundaries meet: ST_Intersection of the boundaries returns the shared lines (and any lone points where they only touch), ST_CollectionExtract(…, 2) keeps the lines and ST_LineMerge joins them into one. The land borders are 267 km (Nicaragua) and 250 km (Panama). Within 10 km of them, on either side, lie 5 and 9 records coded for Costa Rica or the neighbour; 7 of the 14 are coded for the neighbour. So close to a line, the code and the outline can each be right or wrong: a 1:50m border is generalized and can be kilometres off, and the disagreements in Spatial joins, g3, are mostly records like these. The ON clause casts every record's geom to geography, so the index on occurrences.geom can't help, and the join reads all 40,395 records for each border (about 0.7 s); Spatial indexes, g5, shows the plan and an index that does help.

Result

Overlaying the ecoregions on Costa Rica gives seven pieces, led by the Isthmian-Atlantic moist forests (17,288 km², 33.7 %). The pieces add up to 49,908 km² of the outline's 51,303, with 2,070 km² in no ecoregion and about 675 km² counted twice: ten pairs of valid pieces overlap, 678.2 km² in all, along borders each ecoregion drew in its own place. A valid layer isn't a coverage, and an area from this overlay carries an error of a few per cent of the country. Costa Rica's land borders are 267 km (Nicaragua) and 250 km (Panama); 14 records lie within 10 km of them, half of them coded for the neighbour.

Exercise

Check

The answer: g4-border's border lines, buffered by 10,000 m on geography, cast back to geometry and cut by the outline (ST_Intersection); then a spatial join of the records to each strip, as in g3.

The strips are 2,673 km² along Nicaragua and 2,289 km² along Panama, 5.2 % and 4.5 % of Costa Rica. A strip is close to its border's length times 10 km (2,670 and 2,500 km²), and smaller where the sea cuts it off at the border's ends or the border winds, so the buffer on its inner bends overlaps itself. The strips hold 2 and 7 records, against g4-border's 5 and 9 on both sides of the line: the other 3 and 2 lie outside Costa Rica's outline. One record in each strip is coded for the neighbour although it lies inside Costa Rica's outline, two of g3's 206 disagreements. within_10km equals records: the buffer's straight segments lost no record here. Had the cut been left out, the strip would reach 10 km into each neighbour as well, and a record there would count for Costa Rica.

Dialects

  • PostgreSQL: ST_Area, ST_Length, ST_Perimeter, ST_Distance, ST_DWithin and ST_Buffer take geography and answer in metres; ST_Buffer also takes 'quad_segs=…' and, on geometry, 'endcap=flat' or 'side=left' for a one-sided strip. ST_Intersection, ST_Difference, ST_SymDifference and ST_Union overlay, through GEOS; ST_Union and ST_Collect are aggregates too (ST_Collect only gathers, with no merge).
  • DuckDB: the spatial extension (not in this course's browser) has ST_Intersection, ST_Difference, ST_Union, ST_Buffer and ST_DWithin on GEOMETRY, in the units of its coordinates; for metres, ST_Transform to a projected system first, or use ST_Distance_Spheroid (latitude first, g1). Without it, the browser's lab has no shapes to overlay.
  • SQL Server: methods on the shape: a.STIntersection(b), a.STDifference(b), a.STUnion(b), g.STBuffer(d) and a.STDistance(b), in metres on geography; geography::UnionAggregate(geom) dissolves rows. There is no STDWithin: write a.STDistance(b) <= d, which a spatial index can serve when it stands alone in WHERE or ON. BufferWithTolerance(d, tolerance, relative) sets how closely the curves are drawn.
