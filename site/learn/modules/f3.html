Concept

Normalized tables (Normalization, d2) keep each fact once, so most questions need several tables at once. A join pairs the rows of two tables wherever a condition, written after ON, is true; aliases (species AS s) keep the column names short and unambiguous. The ERD on the Schema page says which columns to join on and what to expect: the line species ||--o{ occurrences reads "one species, zero or more records", so joining a species to its records turns one row into many, and countries |o--o{ occurrences says a record has at most one country, possibly none.

  • An inner join (JOIN) keeps only the pairs that match: a species with no records disappears from the result without a word.
  • A left join (LEFT JOIN) keeps every row of the left table; where nothing matches, the right table's columns are NULL. Count a column of the right table (count(o.id)), not count(*), to get 0 for those rows. A condition on the right table belongs in ON: in WHERE it throws away the rows the left join kept, because their NULLs fail it.
  • An anti join keeps the left rows that have no match: LEFT JOIN … WHERE <right key> IS NULL, or WHERE NOT EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM … WHERE <match>). Avoid NOT IN (subquery) for this: if the subquery returns one NULL, NOT IN is never true and the answer is empty.
  • A many-to-many relationship (an animal is recorded in many countries, a country holds many animals) needs a junction table of pairs, here species_countries; you join through it.
  • A join multiplies rows: joining one species to its 20 countries and its 2,000 records gives 40,000 rows, and every count over them is wrong. Join one "many" table at a time, or aggregate before joining (Window functions, f4).

Result

Brazil has 12 animals in critwire's list and 14 in the sample; the two extra ones each rest on one stray record that critwire's rule leaves out. Five animals have no record in the sample, four of them for reasons range_status gives and one because of a dataset's license. A condition on the right table in WHERE turns a left join into an inner one (14 countries become 9), and joining two "many" tables at once turned 2,000 records into 40,000 rows. A degree box is cheap and works anywhere, but it is not an area (a 10° box near the poles is much smaller than one at the equator) and one outlier stretches it: the GIS in SQL modules (Coordinate reference systems, g1, and Spatial joins, g3, both in preparation) do this properly with geometries.

Dialects

  • PostgreSQL: as here; also JOIN … USING (col), FULL JOIN, and LATERAL (a subquery that reads the row beside it). There is no ANTI JOIN keyword: write NOT EXISTS, which the planner runs as an anti join.
  • DuckDB (this page's engine): as PostgreSQL, plus SEMI JOIN and ANTI JOIN keywords, ASOF JOIN (nearest earlier match, for times) and POSITIONAL JOIN.
  • SQL Server: no USING or NATURAL; CROSS APPLY and OUTER APPLY do what LATERAL does; EXCEPT and NOT EXISTS as here; GROUP BY repeats the expressions, not their aliases; floor as here.
